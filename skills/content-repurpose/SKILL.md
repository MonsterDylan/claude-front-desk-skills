# Content repurpose

The questions patients ask the front desk every week are the best content a practice has. Real people already want those answers. You turn a raw question list into posts the doctor can film in a few minutes.

## Before you start
- Use the practice profile: doctor's name, voice, services, words to use and avoid.
- **Strip anything that identifies a patient.** If the list includes names, dates, phone numbers or details that point to one person, remove them and say you did. Keep only the question.

## Output
1. **Ranked list:** group duplicate and near-duplicate questions, count how often each came up, and rank them. Show the top ten at most.
2. **Posts:** for the top three (or the number staff ask for), give:
   - **Hook:** the first line on screen and out loud, under 10 words. Use the patient's own phrasing when you can ("Is NAD+ worth it?").
   - **Script:** 30 to 45 seconds of talking points for the doctor (about 80 to 120 words), in their voice. Answer the question honestly, including "it depends" when that's true, and end with one next step (book, call, ask at your visit).
   - **Caption:** 2 to 4 short lines.
   - **Format:** talking head, carousel, or quick tip, with one line on why.
   - **Check before posting:** any claim the doctor should confirm, and any line that could read as a promise of results.
3. **Next week:** two questions worth asking more patients about to fill gaps.

## Which questions become posts
- Rank by how often they came up. Break ties in favor of the question that can be answered honestly without pricing or a promise of results.
- A personal medical question (about one patient's medication, dose or results) never becomes a post. Keep it in the ranking and tell staff to route it to the clinical team.
- A topic outside the practice's services can still be an education post. Flag it under "Check before posting" so the doctor decides.
- Show fewer than ten when there are fewer distinct questions. Never pad.

## Rules
- Education, not medical advice: no personal recommendations, no dosing, no "this will fix", no guaranteed outcomes, no before-and-after claims.
- Stay inside the practice's services and the doctor's expertise from the profile.
- Plain language. No hype words ("game-changer", "miracle", "secret").
