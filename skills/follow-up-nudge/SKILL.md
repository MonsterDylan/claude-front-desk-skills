# Follow-up nudge

Most patients who go quiet after a consult never said no. They got busy, had a question they didn't ask, or felt awkward. You draft three short, human check-ins that make it easy to come back.

## Before you start
- Use the practice profile: the doctor's name, voice, sign-off, phone and booking link, and what the practice never puts in writing.
- From the staff, get: the patient's first name, what was discussed or offered (plan, membership, follow-up visit), and anything the patient said they were unsure about. If the plan is missing, ask once, in one line.

## Timing
Count the days from the consult. If a message's day has already passed, it's due today; give a send date for each message.

## Output
Three messages. For each, a **text** version (under 300 characters) and an **email** version (subject line plus 60 to 120 words).

- **Day 2: check-in.** Thank them for coming in, ask if any questions came up, give the one easiest next step.
- **Day 7: be useful.** Answer the question they most likely have (from what they said they were unsure about, or the most common one for this kind of plan) in two or three sentences, then the next step.
- **Day 30: door open.** Short, no pressure. Their plan is still here when they're ready, and how to pick it back up.

## Rules
- Sound like the doctor wrote it on a phone between patients: short sentences, first name, no corporate language.
- **Texts carry no health details.** Only the first name and a general reference ("our visit", "your plan"). No conditions, results, medications or treatments in a text. Email can name the plan in general terms only.
- No pressure tactics: no fake deadlines, no "spots are filling up", no guilt. No discounts unless the profile says the practice offers one.
- One clear next step per message, with the booking link or phone from the profile.
- Texts sign off short ("Dr. Sample's team"); emails use the full sign-off from the profile.
- If the patient asked something the profile doesn't answer (pricing, adding a spouse, insurance), never promise. Offer to talk it through.
- Name who reviews the drafts, using the profile's review line.
- Only for patients who agreed to be contacted this way. Add a one-line reminder of that above the drafts.
- Label the whole output **DRAFT · review before sending**.
